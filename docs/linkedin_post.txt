💨 CFD for car aerodynamics takes 8-72 hours per run. I built a surrogate that does it in milliseconds — with uncertainty bounds.

POST:
Aerodynamic drag directly impacts fuel efficiency and EV range. But each Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulation of a full vehicle body can take 8–72 hours on an HPC cluster.

In this project, I trained a Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) surrogate on 600 synthetic CFD evaluations of a DrivAer-style sedan body, predicting the drag coefficient (Cd) from 8 geometric parameters.

What makes GPR special for surrogate modeling:
🔵 Native uncertainty quantification — the model knows when it's uncertain
🔵 Excellent interpolation in smooth design spaces
🔵 Bayesian framework — perfectly suited for coupling with optimization

Results:
✅ R² = 0.997 on unseen test points
✅ RMSE = 0.00256 Cd units
✅ Parametric studies in seconds

Key insight: Windshield angle and rear deck angle are the strongest drivers of Cd — validated by automotive aerodynamics literature (SAE 2022-01-0691).
